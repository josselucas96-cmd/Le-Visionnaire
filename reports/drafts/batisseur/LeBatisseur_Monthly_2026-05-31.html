Investment Objective

Le Bâtisseur is an unconstrained equity paper portfolio focused on quality compounding and capital allocation discipline. The core targets high-grade businesses, exceptional capital allocators, and category leaders, paired with a tactical layer of thematic plays and select opportunities. The portfolio is designed for long-term capital growth with rigorous risk management.

Market Commentary
  • May was an exceptional month for US equities. The S&P 500 gained 5.3% and closed a run of nine consecutive weekly gains, something that has happened only four other times in forty years. The Nasdaq 100 added 10.6% and the Dow crossed 51,000 for the first time.
  • Earnings did the work. With roughly 97% of the S&P 500 reported, about 85% beat estimates against a five-year average of 78%, and AI-linked revenue became a visible driver of index-level profit growth rather than a story about a handful of companies.
  • Rates moved the other way. Following the month's inflation prints the market began assigning better-than-even odds to a Fed hike at the December meeting, the 30-year Treasury yield rose above 5% for the first time since 2007 and the 10-year set a 52-week high. A melt-up in equities and a repricing of policy risk happened in the same four weeks.
Management Commentary
  • Le Bâtisseur was funded on 6 May and closes its first, partial month at +0.96% from the opening capital, with net asset value at $1,009,613. Cash at month-end: 5.45%. Twenty-six days is not a performance record and nothing here should be read as one.
  • No trades. The book was built in a single operation at inception and left alone. The Investment Policy Statement calls for quality compounders and disciplined capital allocators held across full cycles; trading in the first three weeks would contradict the mandate before it has been tested.
  • Attribution. Constellation Software added 1.09pp, Eli Lilly 0.60pp and Fair Isaac 0.52pp. Boston Scientific cost 0.55pp, Alibaba 0.52pp and MercadoLibre 0.40pp. No single name moved the portfolio by more than about one point, which is the intended behaviour of a twenty-eight-line book.
  • Positioning. Realised volatility was 12.1% annualised and beta to the S&P 500 was 0.25. In a month where the index rose 5.3% on the back of its largest technology weights, a low-beta quality book was always going to lag, and it did. The construction is doing what it was designed to do; whether that is the right design is a question for several years, not for May.
  • Note on attribution: Le Bâtisseur started mid-month, so contributions are measured from the closing prices of 6 May. The difference with the month's return is the move between the prices at which positions were opened and that first close.
