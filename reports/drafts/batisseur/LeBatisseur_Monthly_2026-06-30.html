Investment Objective

Le Bâtisseur is an unconstrained equity paper portfolio focused on quality compounding and capital allocation discipline. The core targets high-grade businesses, exceptional capital allocators, and category leaders, paired with a tactical layer of thematic plays and select opportunities. The portfolio is designed for long-term capital growth with rigorous risk management.

Market Commentary
  • US equities declined in June. The S&P 500 lost 1.0% and the Nasdaq Composite 2.8%, while the Russell 2000 gained 3.7%. The largest index constituents underperformed the rest of the market.
  • Inflation data re-accelerated during the month and the Federal Reserve's communication became more restrictive, which weighed on long-duration growth stocks. Software stocks sold off on concerns that agentic AI could reduce spending on traditional enterprise applications.
  • Industrials (+7.2%) and Health Care (+6.5%) were the strongest sectors, Communication Services (-7.9%) and Energy (-5.1%) the weakest.
Management Commentary
  • Le Bâtisseur returned -5.75% in June against -1.03% for the S&P 500, a difference of -4.72 percentage points. The portfolio was fully invested at month-end.
  • Ferrari was sold on 4 June, with a gain of 7.5% since inception, and Broadcom, ServiceNow and Mastercard were opened. VICI Properties was sold on 10 June, with a gain of 0.6% since inception, and CoStar was opened. The position in ServiceNow was increased on 12 June.
  • The underperformance came almost entirely from the software and internet holdings, which represent about a third of the portfolio. Microsoft fell 17%, ServiceNow 17% from its opening level after a 41% rise in May, Zoom 15% and Constellation Software 8%, as the market weighed the effect of agentic AI on software budgets; together these cost about 1.9 points against the index. Alibaba fell 23% to a 52-week low after allegations that it had used fraudulent accounts to extract outputs from a US AI model, and after a price target cut; with Amazon (-12%) and Meta (-11%), the internet holdings cost a further 2.1 points. Circle fell 45%, including 17% on 30 June after the announcement of Open USD, a fee-free stablecoin backed by more than 140 companies including Visa and Google, costing 0.9 points.
  • The index's gains came from Industrials, Health Care and Financials. The portfolio's holdings in these sectors performed well, with Eli Lilly up 9%, JPMorgan 9%, Visa 5% and Novo Nordisk 5%, but they represent a smaller share of the portfolio and were partly offset by Boston Scientific (-12%) and EssilorLuxottica (-9%).
