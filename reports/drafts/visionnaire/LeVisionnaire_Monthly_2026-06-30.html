Investment Objective

Le Visionnaire is an unconstrained international equity paper portfolio focused on high-conviction, concentrated positions. It aims for long-term outperformance of the Nasdaq 100 over rolling 5-year horizons, through rigorous selection of companies that compound shareholder value through innovation and structural differentiation.

Market Commentary
  • US equities gave ground in June while breadth improved. The S&P 500 lost 1.0% and the Nasdaq Composite 2.8%, but the Russell 2000 gained 3.7%: the month punished the largest index weights and rewarded most of what sits below them.
  • Inflation re-accelerated and the Fed's tone hardened with it. June was spent repricing the path of policy rather than the earnings outlook, which weighs most on long-duration growth names — the part of the market this portfolio is built from.
  • Dispersion between sectors was unusually wide: Industrials (+7.2%) and Health Care (+6.5%) led, Communication Services (-7.9%) and Energy (-5.1%) lagged. Bitcoin fell roughly 20% on record spot-ETF outflows, which matters directly to the two crypto-linked lines the book carries.
Management Commentary
  • Le Visionnaire closes June at -2.97% versus -0.15% for the Nasdaq 100 — alpha of -2.82pp. Cash at month-end: 4.54%. Realised volatility was 36.8% annualised with a beta of 0.90 to the benchmark: the shortfall came from single-name risk, not from index exposure.
  • Rotation #3 (1 June). Amazon was closed in full at $265.23, an 8% position, and the proceeds opened Zeta Global at $24.52 (4.9% of NAV). Capital moved from a mega-cap whose weight had grown faster than the thesis behind it, into a smaller marketing-data name where, on my thesis, the operating leverage is still ahead rather than behind.
  • Reinforcements (12 June). Zeta was added to at $19.57, three weeks after the opening purchase, and Metaplanet at $1.53. Both were additions into price weakness with the underlying thesis unchanged, and both concentrate the book further into its two most recent convictions — a deliberate choice, and the one that will be judged first if either thesis is wrong.
  • Attribution. Hims & Hers added 3.21pp on its own and Nebius 1.28pp. Against them, Rocket Lab cost 1.53pp, Celsius 1.33pp, Metaplanet 1.13pp, Zeta 0.95pp and Meta 0.89pp. Twenty concentrated positions behave this way: two strong contributors do not always cover five detractors, and the month is the cost of that construction rather than an accident of it.
